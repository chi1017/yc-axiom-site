Guide / Opening a shop

Online checklist before opening a new shop.
Google Business Profile, website, LINE or Instagram first?

Opening a shop About 8 min read Updated: 2026/11/12

Opening a shop involves a lot, and online details often get left until last. Then on opening day customers cannot find the shop and the map shows no opening hours. This guide walks through what to prepare online before opening, in the recommended order, what each step needs and what to watch for, with a checklist for the week before you open.

Step 1: Settle your basic business details

Every platform uses the same details, so put them in one document first and copy from it everywhere. That way you avoid writing the address one way here and another way there. Search engines and AI compare details across platforms, and consistent details earn more trust.

  • Business name (the official name, written the same way everywhere)
  • Address (including floor) and phone number
  • Opening hours and days off
  • A one-line introduction: area + type of shop + what makes it special
  • Your logo and 5 to 10 photos: the entrance, the interior, your main products or services

Step 2: Set up a Google Business Profile

When people search your name or “cafés near me”, the first thing they usually see is your listing on Google Maps. Set it up before opening, because verification takes a while.

  • Follow the on-screen steps to verify. The verification method depends on what Google offers at the time.
  • Choose the most specific category, for example “Café” rather than “Restaurant”.
  • Fill in your hours, photos and services. You can also set your opening date in advance.

Step 3: Open a LINE Official Account

Customers in Taiwan are used to booking, asking questions and receiving offers on LINE. A LINE Official Account can send automatic replies (hours, address, FAQs). The free plan has a monthly message limit, which is usually enough when you first open.

Step 4: Build your website

Your website is where complete information that rarely changes lives: your menu or prices, services, FAQs and directions. It is also the link you put on your Google Business Profile and in your Instagram bio. Compared with social media alone, a website is easier for Google and AI search to understand.

If time is tight while opening, you can launch a one-page site with website rental and add more later. For what each industry should include, see industry websites.

Step 5: Instagram and Facebook

Social media is great for building excitement before opening: the fit-out, test dishes, opening offers. Put your website and LINE link in your bio so interested people know where to find full details and how to contact you.

Checklist for the week before opening

  1. Search your shop name on your phone and check that your Google Business Profile is correct and your photos show up.
  2. Check that your name, address, phone number and hours match exactly on every platform.
  3. Open your website on a phone and make sure the map, phone and LINE buttons all work.
  4. Send a test message to your LINE Official Account auto-reply.
  5. Make sure the links in your Instagram and Facebook bios open.
  6. Plan how you will ask customers for Google reviews after opening, for example a QR code for the review link on each table.

After opening

The first three months are when reviews and search visibility build up. When your hours change, update Google Business Profile, your website and LINE together. Check your Google Business Profile views and website traffic once a month to see where customers are finding you.

FAQ

Can I set up a Google Business Profile before opening?

Yes, and it is best to start early because verification takes time. You can also set an opening date so customers see your information before you open.

On a tight budget, can the website wait?

You can start with the Google Business Profile and LINE, but don’t wait too long after opening. The website does not need to be big at first. A one-page site with your hours, menu or services and directions already cuts down on customers messaging to ask.

Should my website and Instagram have the same content?

Your basic details should match. Otherwise, split the work: the website holds complete information that rarely changes, and Instagram covers news, events and everyday updates.
