Industry / Massage, Spa & Aromatherapy

Massage studio websites
Earn customers’ trust first, then the booking.

On a first visit to a massage or spa studio, customers care less about the decor than about feeling safe: what the treatment involves, how much it costs, whether the business is reputable, whether the space is clean. This page covers what massage, spa and aromatherapy studio websites should include, and shares a real project of ours.

See a spa sample siteCheck your current website for free

What customers want to know before booking a massage

Massage and spa services depend on trust. Customers want to know the pressure and length, whether they will be upsold, whether they need to change, and whether they can choose a therapist’s gender. If each question has to be asked by message, many people give up halfway.

Clearly listing treatments, prices, photos of the space and policies on your website tells customers you are reputable and transparent. That is more convincing than any adjective.

What a massage or spa website needs

Treatments and prices
The length, technique, who it suits and the price of each treatment, written out separately.
Interior photos
Reception, treatment rooms, showers and changing areas, so customers can see it is clean and private.
Therapist profiles
Specialties, certifications or training, so customers know who will be looking after them.
Booking and rescheduling policies
How to book, what happens if you are late and the cancellation policy. Writing it down reduces no-shows.
Policies
Which conditions are not suitable, how long to wait after a meal, whether pregnant customers can book. It protects customers and the business.
Directions and parking
Studios are often in residential buildings or alleys, so explain clearly how to find you and get upstairs.

The three most common massage studio website problems

  1. 01
    Treatment names with no explanation

    Names like “deep relaxation” or “meridian balancing” are hard to compare. Give the length, technique and who it suits.

  2. 02
    Overblown wording

    Words about curing or treating conditions make people suspicious and may raise regulatory issues. Plain descriptions work better.

  3. 03
    An entrance nobody can find

    If customers reach your building and still have to call for directions, the first impression already suffers.

Massage & spa website FAQ

01. Does a massage studio need a website, or is LINE enough? +
LINE is great for bookings and messages, but new customers usually find you by searching Google for your area plus “massage”. A website lets first-time visitors understand your treatments and prices before adding you on LINE, so the enquiries you get are better.
02. Can the website take bookings? +
Website rental includes a booking form: customers choose a date, time and treatment, the request goes to your email, and you reply once you confirm. The Advanced plan adds a booking calendar where you set how many people each time slot can take.
03. Can we sell treatment packages or gift vouchers? +
Yes, with the online checkout feature. Payments go straight to your own ECPay account.
04. Can the website talk about health benefits? +
We advise against it. Describe the technique, length and how relaxing it feels, and avoid words like “treatment” or “cure”. Customers also find that more credible.
