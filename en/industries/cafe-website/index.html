Café websites
Help customers choose you before they leave home.

When people search for a café nearby, they decide within seconds whether to tap in: are you open today, is there a seat, can they bring a laptop, what does the menu look like. This page covers what a coffee shop, bakery or dessert shop website should include, the most common mistakes, and the difference between website rental and a custom website.

What customers are really checking when they look for a café

Most people search on their phone, before heading out or when they are already nearby. They do not want a long brand story. They want practical answers: are you open now, should they book for the weekend, are there outlets and Wi-Fi, can they bring a pet, are today’s desserts still available.

If that information lives only in Instagram posts or stories, customers have to scroll back through them one by one, and Google Maps hours are often out of date. Your own website puts these answers in one place that you can change any time and search engines can read.

What a café website needs

Menu and prices
List items and prices as text, not just a photo of the menu. Search engines can read text, and it is easier to read on a phone.
Opening hours and temporary closures
Write each day’s hours separately. Days off, holidays and typhoon closures should be quick to change and show up on the home page right away.
Interior photos
The bar, seating, window seats and where the outlets are. Customers want to know if it suits chatting, reading or working.
Reservations and private events
If you fill up on weekends, a booking or private-event form works better than going back and forth in Instagram messages.
Map and directions
Add a Google map and the nearest parking or bus stop. Shops tucked in alleys especially need “which corner to turn at”.
FAQ
Minimum spend, time limits, laptops, pets, takeaway beans. Answer the questions you get every day once.

The three most common café website problems

  1. 01
    The menu is just a photo

    Search engines cannot read words in an image, so someone searching “Taichung Basque cheesecake” will not find you.

  2. 02
    Hours that don’t match Google Maps

    Customers who make a wasted trip often leave a bad review. Update the website and the map together.

  3. 03
    A beautiful site that’s slow on phones

    Uncompressed photos take several seconds to load on mobile data, and customers go somewhere else.

Café website FAQ

01. Is Instagram enough for a café? +
Instagram is great for regulars and new items, but posts get buried and search engines barely read them. A website holds the information that stays the same: menu, hours, directions and FAQs. Use both: Instagram gets you noticed, the website helps people decide to come.
02. Our prices change often. Is updating the menu a hassle? +
The website rental editor works on your phone. Change prices, add seasonal items or mark something sold out today, and the live site updates about one to two minutes after you publish.
03. Can customers reserve a table on the website? +
Yes. Website rental includes a booking form: customers choose a date, time and party size, the request goes to your email, and you reply once you confirm. Shops that need time slots managed automatically can use the booking calendar on the Advanced plan.
04. Can we sell coffee beans or gift boxes? +
Yes. Website rental has built-in online checkout, and payments go straight to your own ECPay account. Before you have ECPay, you can still show products and take orders by phone or LINE.
